# Voice Configuration

This public edition has no embedded personal voice profile or client examples.

## Use the user's preferences

Apply the requested audience, point of view, language, formality, directness, humor, formatting, and words to avoid. When supplied, use approved writing samples to infer cadence and vocabulary without copying their private factual content. Preferences apply within this task unless the user explicitly asks to save them.

If a voice decision matters and no evidence establishes it, ask one focused question. Otherwise use the neutral defaults and do not claim a precise imitation.

## Neutral defaults

- Plain language, concrete verbs, and natural contractions where appropriate.
- A calm, direct, useful tone; varied sentence lengths and readable paragraphs.
- Claims connected to reasons or observable mechanisms.
- Confidence proportionate to the evidence; retain meaningful uncertainty.
- Technical terminology when useful, with an explanation suited to the audience.
- Minimal decorative formatting, no forced humor, and no engagement bait.

These are starting preferences, not universal rules. A quotation, technical term, deliberate stylistic choice, or requested format should not be altered just to match a checklist.

## Preserve the source

Retain the user's intended point, qualifications, supporting facts, and genuine personality. Do not add personal history, status, credentials, or experiences. Do not invent quotations, customer outcomes, figures, chronology, or causality. Keep approved source wording when it works.

Avoid generic hooks that the body cannot support. Prefer the mechanism or useful observation over self-promotion. Stop when the useful point is complete; do not force a slogan or question ending.

## Check the draft

Would the user recognize the position and tone? Does each paragraph advance the same argument? Are sources and uncertainty preserved? Is the writing natural for the destination without pretending to be someone whose voice was never supplied?
